Why CFD firms are rushing into futures

US traders mostly can't trade CFDs, but they can trade CME futures, and futures prop searches are growing faster than forex. So the big CFD brands are following the traders. That's good news for you: more competition has pushed fees down, and several new programs use end-of-day drawdown instead of the harsher intraday kind.

FTMO Futures: the one to watch

This is the launch I'm most interested in. FTMO has paid me on its CFD challenges in 2–3 days, and its futures program uses the rule I care most about: an end-of-day trailing drawdown that stops rising once it reaches your starting balance. That applies even during the evaluation, which is unusual.

The catches are real, though. It's a monthly subscription (reported $119 to $269), positions are force-closed at 16:10 ET, and as a beta product it has no payout history yet. FTMO's CFD record earns it the benefit of the doubt, but I'd still wait for payout reports before buying a big account.

My rules for any new firm

After 50+ evaluations, here's how I approach a new firm or program:

  • Drawdown first. On futures I only buy end-of-day trailing; intraday cost me Apex accounts again and again. On CFDs I only trade static.
  • Payout proof before size. Hype is not a payout record. Wait until real traders post certificates.
  • Start with the smallest account. Get one payout, then scale.
  • Trust brands that have paid you elsewhere. A new program from a firm with years of payouts is a smaller bet than a brand-new firm.

Should you switch to a new firm?

Only if it fixes a problem you actually have. If you're getting paid by an established firm, a new launch's lower fee isn't worth the risk. If you're a US trader who wanted FTMO or The5ers but couldn't trade CFDs, their futures programs are worth a small test account, once payout reports start coming in.
