CFD or futures: which suits gold?

You can trade gold two ways at prop firms. Futures (GC, or the smaller MGC) trade on COMEX with exchange hours and a daily break. CFDs (XAUUSD) are priced from the firm's own broker feed, so quotes, spreads and gaps can differ from firm to firm (third-party explanation). The same chart level may not fill the same way at two CFD firms.

  • Futures firms limit you with a contract cap and a drawdown. Costs are commissions per contract. The drawdown on a 50K is usually $2,000, and the type is usually end-of-day trailing.
  • CFD firms limit you with a percentage daily loss and a maximum loss, plus leverage. Costs are mostly spread, and I did not find a published gold spread for any CFD firm (FTMO shows live spreads only inside its platform). So I print no spread figures; check the XAUUSD symbol in your platform.

My own rules: on CFD firms I only trade static drawdown, and on futures I only buy end-of-day trailing. Neither route is better for everyone. Futures give you exchange-traded contracts you can size in 10-ounce steps; CFD firms give a percentage floor.

Contract basics, and what I could not verify

Third-party sources (including FundedNext's own blog and Topstep's brokerage blog) give GC as 100 troy ounces, $0.10 per tick, so $10 a tick or $100 a point. MGC is 10 ounces, $1 a tick, $10 a point, so 10 MGC equal 1 GC. I could not read CME's contract pages, so treat these as third-party figures and check them at CME.

For gold CFDs, a "standard lot" of 100 ounces is common at retail brokers, but I did not find the lot size for FTMO, FundedNext or Crypto Fund Trader. The CFD arithmetic below therefore assumes 100 ounces per 1.0 lot and is only as good as that assumption.

I state no margin figure. No firm I checked publishes one for gold; futures firms use contract caps and drawdown, and CFD firms use leverage. Gold leverage is unresolved at every CFD firm here (see the CFD section).

Gaps, news and weekends

Gold futures stop trading for a daily break, and prop firms add their own flat-by times. A price can gap across that break or across the Sunday reopen, and a stop does not protect you from a gap. On a futures account with a $2,000 floor and 4 contracts, a 5-point gap is the whole limit. On CFDs, the gap you see depends on the firm's broker feed.

FAQ

Should I trade GC or MGC on a prop account?

That's your call. Using third-party tick values, MGC is one tenth of GC ($10 a point against $100), so the same loss limit gives ten times the points of room per contract. Check the specs on CME's site, which I couldn't read.

Is gold better on a CFD firm or a futures firm?

Neither is better outright. CFD firms give a percentage drawdown (static at the ones I use) and you pay spread; futures firms give a dollar drawdown and contract caps and you pay commission. Compare the arithmetic above.
