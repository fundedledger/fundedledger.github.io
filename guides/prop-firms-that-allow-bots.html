Prop Firms That Allow Trading Bots in 2026

Most prop firms say they allow EAs and bots. Almost all of them ban certain kinds, and many restrict bots you didn't write yourself. Here's where automation is welcome, where it isn't, and what that means if you ever sell a bot.

Automation policies are among the most frequently changed rules, and 2026 sources disagree on several firms. Get the firm's policy in writing (a support ticket is fine) before running a bot on a funded account.

What almost every firm bans

  • High-frequency trading and tick scalping
  • Latency or price-feed arbitrage: exploiting delays in the firm's data
  • Hedge arbitrage across accounts or firms
  • Martingale and grid systems at many firms
  • Black-box "prop-pass" bots: commercial bots built to pass challenges

Thinking of selling your bot? Read this first

This is the riskiest case. When many traders run the same bot, firms see identical trades across unrelated accounts and can flag every one of them. Several firms ban it outright:

  • Bulenox bans commercial, shared, rented or publicly distributed automation.
  • E8 Markets bans the same strategy across users.
  • FundedNext bans commercial challenge-passing bots and caps capital per unique strategy.
  • The5ers bans coordinated trading with other traders.
  • Tradeify wants proof that you wrote and run the code yourself.

So a bot marketed as "pass any prop challenge" puts your buyers' accounts at risk, and your reputation with them. Cleaner ways to sell a strategy:

  • Sell it for personal brokerage accounts, not prop accounts, and say so clearly.
  • Sell indicators or signals rather than a fully automated bot, for example as an invite-only script on TradingView.
  • Tell buyers the prop firm rules up front, including which firms ban shared bots.

Selling trading software can also raise legal issues, especially around performance claims and marketing. Talk to a lawyer before you launch.
