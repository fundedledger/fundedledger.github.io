Trade Copiers and VPS for Prop Traders

What each firm says about copying trades and running a VPS, what Tradesyncer and QuantVPS cost on paper, and where each can go wrong.

Updated October 2026Written by a funded trader

Disclosure: some links on this page are affiliate links. If you buy through them I may earn a commission, at no extra cost to you.

Short answer: copy trades only between accounts you own, check your firm's current rule in writing before you start, and accept that if the copier fails, it's your risk, not the firm's. Most prop traders don't need a VPS. I place my Lucid orders from TradingView through Tradovate. A VPS is for unattended bots and desktop copiers that need a PC running all day, and Topstep prohibits VPS use. Free native copiers (Tradovate Group Trading, the TopstepX copier) cover many cases before you pay for anything.

A copier failure is your loss. If a stop-loss doesn't copy, an order duplicates or a connection drops, the firm won't refund the damage. Lucid's page says traders are "fully responsible for any software errors, malfunctions, or unintended outcomes". A copied mistake can end several accounts at once.

Do you need a copier or a VPS?

  1. One account, trading by handNo. Neither tool adds anything.
  2. Several of your own accountsMaybe a copier. Try the free native one your platform offers first.
  3. A bot or NinjaTrader strategy that runs unattendedA VPS can keep it running away from home internet or power cuts, if your firm allows automation and VPS. See prop firms that allow bots.
  4. A desktop copier that needs your PC onA VPS is the usual fix. Tradesyncer's pitch is that its copying runs in the cloud, so no VPS is needed.

Latency matters mostly to scalpers, and prop firms ban high-frequency trading anyway.

What gets accounts banned: other people, signals and hedging

The line firms draw is ownership. Copying your own accounts is usually fine. Copying anyone else's trades is where accounts get terminated.

  • Between different people: Tradeify says copying "from or to accounts owned by other people is strictly prohibited and may result in account termination".
  • Signal groups and group trading: FundedNext's Futures page says traders "cannot group trade or coordinate trades with other traders" and cannot "subscribe to any trading signal services or use external signals". It says this holds even through automated tools or copiers.
  • Shared bots: some firms restrict running the same strategy across different users. E8 does, and Bulenox restricts shared tools; see the bots guide.
  • Hedging across accounts: a copier that opens opposite positions on two accounts, even at different firms, can breach a hedging rule. Lucid has a separate prohibited-hedging article, and Apex's page says its hedging rule still applies to copying.

Mirroring a third party's trades is the riskiest use of a copier. Don't do it.

Tradesyncer

What it is: a cloud trade copier. You pick a leader account and follower accounts, set a size multiplier per follower (including mini-to-micro mapping), and bracket orders (take-profit and stop-loss) copy and update. The vendor says it supports Tradovate, Rithmic, NinjaTrader and others, and names Apex, Lucid, Topstep, Tradeify and MFFU among 50+ firms and platforms. Naming a firm isn't a statement that the firm allows copying: see the table above.

One third-party aggregator calls it "still in beta" and says liability is capped at one month's subscription; I couldn't check that against Tradesyncer's terms. On a funded account a missed stop can end the account. Run a trial on a demo or small account first and check every fill. Set a reminder to cancel before day 7.

Free alternatives

Tradovate has Group Trading and TopstepX has its own copier, both free per the platforms' help pages. They only cover accounts on that platform, and the TopstepX copier is limited to Combine and Express Funded accounts. You can also run a desktop copier on your own PC, as long as you keep it on and watched. If these cover your setup, you may not need a paid tool.

Checklist before you copy anything

  1. Own every account.All of them in your name, at every firm involved.
  2. Get the rule in writing.Ask support by email or chat, and keep the reply. Do this especially at Topstep for any cloud or VPS tool.
  3. Check hedging.Make sure the copier can never open opposite positions across accounts.
  4. Test small.Use demo or your smallest account and compare every fill, stop and close.
  5. Mind the drawdown.Copying multiplies a loss across accounts. See trailing vs end-of-day drawdown.

FAQ

Is copy trading allowed at prop firms?

Often yes between accounts you own, but firm by firm and sometimes account type by account type. Copying between different people is banned or restricted at several firms. Confirm on the firm's help centre.

Do I need a VPS to trade futures at a prop firm?

Most traders don't. If you place orders by hand, home internet is enough. A VPS mainly helps unattended bots and desktop copiers, and Topstep prohibits it.

Does Topstep allow Tradesyncer or QuantVPS?

I can't tell you. Topstep's page prohibits VPS and remote servers and bars servers from relaying orders for you; it doesn't say how a cloud copier is treated. Ask Topstep support in writing and keep the answer.

Can I copy a signal group's trades to my prop account?

That's the use most likely to get an account terminated. FundedNext says traders cannot use external signals, and Tradeify prohibits copying to or from other people's accounts.

Is Tradesyncer's free trial really free?

It's a 7-day trial with full access, but a card is required and it's charged on day 7 unless you cancel before then, per its pricing page.
