How the math works

  1. Best day share = best day ÷ total profit. $1,500 ÷ $4,000 = 37.5%.
  2. You pass if the share is at or under the limit. At a 40% limit, 37.5% passes.
  3. Profit needed = best day ÷ limit. $1,500 ÷ 0.40 = $3,750 total, so any total at or above that passes with the same best day.
  4. Biggest best day allowed = total × limit. On $4,000 that is $1,600.

Firms word this differently. Some measure against total profit, some against profit since the last payout, some use different percentages for different account types, and some firms have no consistency rule at all. This calculator uses the simple version above. Your firm's own rule page is the one that counts. To see how the rule works in plain English, read the prop firm rules cheat sheet and how challenges work.

If you fail it

The usual fix is to keep trading with normal size until the total grows, not to chase a bigger number. Raising total profit with the same best day lowers your share. Trading bigger to do it can break other rules, like the daily loss limit or drawdown. If you want to compare how firms treat this rule, see our prop firm deals and reviews.
