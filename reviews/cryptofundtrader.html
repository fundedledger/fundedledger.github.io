The rules that matter, explained

Overnight and weekend holding are allowed, there's no fixed maximum lot size, and the banned list (high-frequency trading, tick scalping, latency arbitrage) won't touch a normal swing or intraday trader. These three are what I think actually end accounts.

1. The 5% daily loss, measured on equity from 12:05 AM UTC

The daily loss limit on the 2-Phase is 5% of your balance at 12:05 AM UTC. Breaches are calculated on equity, which means open trades count, not just closed ones. This is the rule I'd tattoo on my screen. A trade you were planning to hold can end your account while it's still open and still recoverable.

Example, not a real account: you hold a 100K 2-Phase account. At 12:05 AM UTC your balance is $100,000, so your daily limit is $5,000. By the London open you've closed a $900 loser, and a second trade is floating $4,200 against you. Your equity is down $5,100 on the day. You've breached, even if price turns and the second trade would have closed at break-even. Everyone knows "don't hold losers", but with an equity-based limit the damage is done before you decide to close.

The practical lesson: set your own daily stop well inside the firm's limit, so a fast move in crypto or gold can't carry you through it. The 1-Phase is tighter still, with a 4% daily limit.

2. Max loss: fixed on the 2-Phase, trailing on the 1-Phase

On the 2-Phase the 10% max loss is fixed off your initial balance. A 100K account has a floor at $90,000 that never moves, however much you make. That's the drawdown type I prefer on CFD firms, and it's why I only bought the 2-Phase here. (Why the drawdown type matters.)

The 1-Phase uses a 6% trailing limit (the Instant account also has a 6% max loss, but the pages don't state its type). For the 1-Phase the firm describes it as balance-based, but its pages don't agree on when it stops trailing. The FAQ's worked example doesn't match a 6% trail (its $94,000 floor on a 100K account would be $101,000 if the trail followed your highs), and the Terms say the floor locks at your opening balance once you've made 6% profit. Confirm the lock point with the firm before you buy a 1-Phase. On a 50K account, a 6% trail is $3,000, and the floor starts at $47,000 and rises with each new high until it locks, if and when it does.

3. The conduct rules: profit cap, reverse trading, copy trading

These are the rules traders say they met only at payout time. I can't verify those stories, and the firm replies to complaints with templated text. But the rules themselves are in the firm's pages, so read them before you size up.

  • A $10,000 cap on simulated profit per day and per trade. The day is measured from 12:10 AM UTC. On small accounts you'll never touch it. On a 200K account a 5% day is exactly $10,000, so a bigger day goes over. I haven't traded an account that big, so I can't tell you how the firm enforces it. Traders on Trustpilot tie it to denials, which are unverified. If you size big, don't let one lucky day sit above the cap.
  • Reverse trading. This one is a conflict. The FAQ says opposite trades are allowed after 24 hours and bans opposite positions held together for 60 seconds or more. The Terms ban "hedging or reverse trading" outright. I'd follow the stricter reading and never hold opposite positions on the same instrument.
  • Copy trading. The firm bans it on Ascend, even between your own accounts, and limits it elsewhere. A Trustpilot reviewer reports a ban over alleged copy trading in September 2026. I haven't verified that. If you use a copier across firms, read this part of the Terms twice and keep each account separate.

Three more points where the pages disagree or confuse people:

  • Minimum trading days. When I did mine there was a 5-day minimum. The FAQ now says there's none for evaluations (Break asks for 1). Older third-party reviews still say 5.
  • News trading. The FAQ and homepage say it's allowed, but Ascend has a 2-minute rule around high-impact news and the market open. Sources differ, so check your program.
  • Leverage. The homepage says up to 1:100 on everything. The FAQ and Terms say 1:100 only on larger 2-Phase and 1-Phase accounts, and 1:30 forex, 1:20 indices and 1:5 crypto on small accounts, Ascend and Instant. Don't size a trade on the homepage number.

The firm's Terms let it decide how rules apply and can override the FAQ. Treat the Terms as the stricter document.

Getting paid

On a 2-Phase or 1-Phase funded account, you can request a payout after 15 traded days or every 30 calendar days. A paid add-on (20% of the account price) cuts that to every 7 traded days. Payouts go out as bank transfer (EUR or USD) or crypto (USDT on ERC20 or TRC20, BTC, ETH). I found no minimum or cap stated. You need to close all trades first, and your ID and proof of address have to be approved before the first withdrawal.

The FAQ says processing takes "approximately 8 hours", with up to 48 business hours for review. In my experience it was 2–3 days from request to payment, every time. Speed was never my problem.

Split: 80% on a funded account, or 90% with the paid add-on. The homepage also shows scaling tables with 50% to 90% splits and no program label, so I can't say which accounts they apply to.

Example, not a real payout: $6,000 of profit on a funded 100K 2-Phase pays $4,800 at 80%. With the 90% add-on (20% of the $660 price, so $132) you'd get $5,400. That's $600 more for $132. The add-on pays for itself once your profit passes $1,320, because $132 is 10% of $1,320.

The catch in the payout section is the legal one. The firm's Terms (section 14.2) say it "does not guarantee the payment of the paid scholarship" and may decline a payout despite achievement. Your only remedy is the return of fees. The firm calls these accounts demo or educational "scholarship" programs, and what you earn is a reward on a simulated account. That's common in this industry, but it is not something I'd gloss over. It's why I say to withdraw early and often.

  • You prefer a fixed max loss and will stay well inside a 5% equity-based daily limit.
  • You'll read the Terms, not just the homepage, and withdraw early rather than leave profit sitting.

FAQ

Does Crypto Fund Trader actually pay?

It has paid me around $15,000, each payout in about 2–3 days, with no denials. That's my experience, and my certificates are from 2023. The firm's Terms say it doesn't guarantee payment, and some traders report denials, so withdraw as soon as you qualify.

Is Crypto Fund Trader legit?

It paid me reliably, but it carries warning signs: Trustpilot suppressed its rating, third parties report a Swiss regulator warning that I couldn't confirm, and the Terms don't guarantee payouts. Treat it as higher trust risk than FTMO, keep account sizes modest and withdraw early.

Is there a minimum number of trading days?

When I did my challenges there was a 5-day minimum per phase. The firm's FAQ now says there is no minimum for evaluations. The Break program asks for 1 day. Check the plan at checkout.

Which program should I pick, 1-Phase or 2-Phase?

I bought the 2-Phase because its 10% max loss is fixed. The 1-Phase uses a smaller 6% trailing limit, and the firm's pages disagree on where the trail locks, so confirm that before buying a 1-Phase.

Can I hold trades overnight and trade the news?

Overnight and weekend holding is allowed. News trading is allowed according to the FAQ, but Ascend has a 2-minute rule around high-impact news and the market open. Sources differ, so check your program.

What is the $10,000 daily profit cap?

The Terms cap simulated profit at $10,000 per day and per trade, with the day measured from 12:10 AM UTC. On a 200K account a 5% day is $10,000, so a bigger day goes over. I haven't traded an account that large, so I can't say how it's enforced.

Can I get a refund if I fail?

Not for a failed challenge or a rule breach. There is a 14-day withdrawal right, but it's lost once you've opened a position, and refunds are at the firm's discretion.
