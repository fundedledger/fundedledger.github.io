Which challenge to pick: the 2-Step

The 2-Step's 10% max loss is static: the floor never moves. The 1-Step and Instant programs use a 6% trailing limit, smaller and harder to keep, and one review notes the firm doesn't clearly say whether the Instant limit trails, so assume the stricter reading. On CFD firms I only trade static drawdown, which is why I've only bought the 2-Step here. (Why the drawdown type matters.)

Expect the 2-Step to take time. With a 5-day minimum per phase, the fastest I've passed a phase is about a week.

What getting funded costs

It took me about three accounts to get my first funded one, which matches my overall pass rate of roughly one in three. Budget for that:

  • Challenge fee × 3 is a realistic cost to get funded, not the price of one attempt.
  • Add-ons: two optional add-ons (such as the 90% split), each costing 20% of the challenge price, aren't shown on the pricing page and can raise the cost by up to 40%.
  • No refunds or reset discounts on evaluation fees.

Getting paid

In my experience each payout took about 2–3 days from request to payment, and none was denied. Sources differ on how often you can withdraw: some describe on-demand payouts after the first one, others a cycle of every 15 active trading days or 30 calendar days. Check the terms for your account.

One review also reports a 40% rule on some funded account types: no single day can be more than 40% of your total profit. Keep your days even-sized and it won't hold you up.

The red flags, and how to weigh them

I hadn't seen these until I researched this review. You should know about them before you buy:

  • Swiss regulator warning. FINMA put cryptofundtrader.com on its public warning list in August 2024, with the reason "not entered in commercial register." The firm says it operates through a Swiss company registered in Zug under a different name. FINMA says a listing doesn't automatically mean an activity is unlawful, but the warning is still up.
  • No Trustpilot score. Trustpilot removed the rating for breaching its guidelines and deleted fake reviews.
  • No independent payout record. The firm says it has paid over $20 million, but no third party has verified it. My own ~$15,000 is one real data point, not an audit.
  • Hidden add-on costs and no refunds, as covered above.

Put together: I've been paid reliably, but you're trusting the firm more than you would FTMO. Protect yourself by keeping account sizes modest and withdrawing as soon as you qualify.
