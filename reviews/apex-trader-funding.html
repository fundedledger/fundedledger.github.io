My experience with Apex

Intraday trailing drawdown cost me Apex accounts again and again, so I stopped buying it. Now, on any futures firm, I only take end-of-day trailing. If you take one thing from this review, take that.

I've bought Apex evaluations, but Apex hasn't paid me out yet. So this review is based on its published rules and my time in its evaluations, not on a payout I've received. Weigh it against the firms that have paid me: FTMO, Lucid Trading and Crypto Fund Trader.

End-of-day or intraday? Pick end-of-day (I learned this the hard way)

With intraday trailing, your limit follows your highest open profit. Take a 50K account with $2,000 of drawdown: a trade that runs to +$1,500 and comes back to +$200 moves your floor up $1,500, leaving you $700 of room after a winning trade. With end-of-day trailing, the floor only moves at the close, to $200 above where it started, and you keep your full $2,000 of room.

That's exactly how intraday trailing kept ending my Apex accounts. Intraday versions are cheaper, but there's no discount big enough to make up for that. (Full drawdown guide.)

What getting funded costs

Apex's list prices look high (end-of-day 50K is reported around $450–590), but it runs coupons constantly, and discounted evaluations often cost a small fraction of list. Then add the activation fee when you pass. At my overall pass rate of roughly one in three:

Never buy Apex at list price. And remember the 30-day clock: if you don't pass in time, that attempt is gone.

The payout rules, explained

Passing is the easy part. To withdraw, you need 5 qualifying trading days per payout, and your best day since the last payout must be under 50% of the profit you've made since then.

Say you've made $4,000 since your last payout and your best day was $2,200. That's 55%, so the payout button stays locked. It isn't a violation; you just keep trading until that best day is under half. Another $500 of steady profit brings you to $4,500, $2,200 is now 49%, and you can request the payout.

Who Apex is for

A good fit: futures day traders with a steady, repeatable edge who can pass within 30 days, buy on a coupon, and grind out consistent days for payouts.

Not a good fit: traders who make most of their profit on a few big days, anyone tempted by the cheaper intraday version, or algo traders.

FAQ

Has Apex paid you?

Not yet. I've bought Apex evaluations and lost accounts to intraday drawdown. This review is based on its rules and my time in its evaluations. The firms that have paid me are FTMO, Lucid Trading and Crypto Fund Trader.

Should I choose end-of-day or intraday drawdown?

End-of-day. Intraday trailing follows your open-profit peaks and ends accounts after winning trades that pull back. It's the reason I stopped buying intraday accounts.

Is there an activation fee?

Yes, on standard accounts, paid once after you pass: reported around $109–159 for end-of-day accounts. Some versions skip it for a higher upfront price.

Why can't I request a payout?

Usually the 50% rule: your best day since the last payout is half or more of your profit since then. Keep trading steady days until it drops below 50%. You also need 5 qualifying days.

Can I use a trading bot on Apex?

A September 2026 update reports automation banned on all accounts. Check the current policy before running anything automated.
