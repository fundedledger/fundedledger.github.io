Reports differ on the profit split and how many payouts an account can take. Check Apex's Help Center for the current numbers before you buy.

End-of-day or intraday drawdown? Pick end-of-day (I learned this the hard way)

With intraday trailing, your limit follows your highest open profit. A trade that runs up and comes back costs you room even if you closed flat. End-of-day trailing only moves at the close, so normal swings inside a trade don't eat into it. Unless you scalp tiny targets and never let trades run, end-of-day is the safer choice. The 50% payout rule, explained

Say you've made $4,000 since your last payout and your best day was $2,200. That's 55%, so the payout button stays locked. It isn't a violation; you just need to keep trading until that best day is under half. Another $500 of steady profit brings you to $4,500, and $2,200 is now 49%, so you can request the payout.

Pros

  • No consistency rule during the evaluation
  • No minimum trading days to pass
  • You choose your drawdown type
  • One-time fee instead of a monthly subscription

Cons

  • 50% consistency check before every payout
  • 5 qualifying days needed per payout
  • 30-day access window: a slow month means paying again
  • Rules changed a lot in 2026, and old guides are wrong

Who Apex is for

A good fit: futures day traders with a steady, repeatable edge who can pass quickly and then grind out consistent days.

Not a good fit: traders who make most of their profit on a few big days, or anyone who needs more than 30 days to hit a target.
