My experience with TradeDay

I haven't traded TradeDay, and it hasn't paid me. I've bought 100+ evals at other firms and passed 40+, so I know what I look for: here's how I'd judge it. Everything below comes from TradeDay's own site and help center, read on 5 November 2026, plus public reviews. It relaunched as "TradeDay 2.0" on 29 May 2026, so older reviews describe a different line-up.

The lens I use: on futures I only buy end-of-day trailing drawdown. Intraday trailing cost me Apex accounts again and again. Even end-of-day has ended funded accounts of mine that were in profit. That is why the funded drawdown type is the first thing I check here. For a futures firm that has paid me, see Lucid Trading.

The rules that matter, explained

TradeDay's FAQ says it "has just one rule": don't break the maximum drawdown. That's true in the evaluation. These are the rules after it that I'd read twice.

1. Quick Pay EOD turns intraday once you're funded

TradeDay's payout policy says the trailing drawdown on all Funded Sim Quick Pay accounts is calculated intraday, and the pricing cards label it "End of Day (Intraday Funded Drawdown)". You pass on one rule and trade on another. A 1-star Trustpilot reviewer complained of exactly this in July 2026; TradeDay replied that it is clearly outlined in its rules.

Example, $50K with a $2,000 drawdown (my numbers, not TradeDay's). The limit starts at $48,000. On end-of-day, a close at $51,000 moves it to $49,000, and a day that peaks higher but closes flat moves nothing. On an intraday trail, say you are up $1,500 at the peak and give it all back to close flat at $50,000: the limit has already trailed to $49,500, so you are $500 from the floor instead of $2,000. Open equity must stay above the active level in both versions.

Fast Pass stays end-of-day when funded, and on your first payout request its drawdown jumps to the starting balance and stays fixed. If you only buy end-of-day, that makes it the one fit. See trailing vs end-of-day drawdown.

2. Consistency: one big day can block you

Example, $50K with the $3,000 target. At 30% (Quick Pay) no single day may exceed $900 when your total is $3,000; a $1,500 day would need $5,000 total. At 45% (Fast Pass) the limit is $1,350, and a $1,500 day needs about $3,334 total. Fast Pass applies 45% again at payout on newer accounts: TradeDay's own example refuses a payout when a $10,000 day makes up about 58.8% of $17,000 gross.

3. The Quick Pay $10,000 cap and the 50/50 split

Quick Pay pays 50/50 while current profit is below $4,000, then 80/20. TradeDay's examples on $50K: profit $3,000 and a $1,000 request pays $500; profit $6,000 and a $3,000 request pays $2,100 (80% of $2,000 plus 50% of $1,000).

The surprise is the cap. Each Quick Pay funded account is reviewed at $10,000 gross profit (profit plus withdrawals). You must stop trading and most traders move to Funded Live. Profit above $10,000 is removed, not paid or transferred, and the homepage cards don't mention it. Example (TradeDay's numbers): gross $10,000 with $3,500 already paid means $6,500 moves over, split $2,500 at 80/20 and $4,000 at 50/50, so $4,000 lands in the Live account with its drawdown at $0. As I read the page, a day that overshoots $10,000 loses the excess. Take payouts along the way.

4. No refunds, and what that means for resets

The Terms say fees are final and non-refundable if you fail, cancel or break the terms, and a bank dispute can get you refused future service. A reset is free if you wait for your next subscription payment. The cards show a reset at the monthly price, but the Terms say $99, so check the checkout.

Other trading rules: bots, copiers, news, hours

  • Bots and copiers: automation only on supported platforms; bots bought from a third party, 200+ trades a day, a VPN or VPS, order splitting and hedging are banned, with profits confiscated (bot guide). Copying from your own accounts is allowed across 2 to 10 accounts, up to 5 on Funded Live.
  • Hours and news: day trading only, with auto-liquidation 10 minutes before the close (funded traders must be flat themselves; if liquidation fails there's no reset). Tier 1 news (FOMC, CPI, NFP and others) is blocked from 2 minutes before to 2 minutes after.
  • Platforms: Tradovate, NinjaTrader, TradingView and Rithmic-based platforms (TradingView guide). Up to 10 accounts at once, on one broker.

Payouts

  • Minimum and eligibility: $250 on both routes. Quick Pay pays from any positive balance in Funded Sim with no buffer and no per-request cap listed. Fast Pass has no buffer but needs a balance above the start, net profit since your last payout and 5 profitable days of at least $100 (25K), $150 (50K), $200 (100K) or $250 (150K).
  • Fast Pass caps: each request is capped at 50% of the account balance and a tier cap. On accounts opened on or after 26 July 2026 that is $750 (25K), $1,500 (50K), $1,875 (100K) and $2,250 (150K). Example: on a new $50K account at 80/20 a capped $1,500 request pays $1,200 before withdrawal fees.
  • Size and Live: Fast Pass funded accounts start at 1 to 4 minis (2 to 15 in the evaluation); by the fifth payout request you move to Funded Live, which pays 90/10 but costs $156 a month per exchange in data plus commissions.
  • Speed and fees: requests before about 5:30 pm Central (one page says Eastern) are processed within 24 hours on business days, paid through Riseworks. International wire $15, US wire free, layer-1 crypto $2.50 plus gas. I haven't been paid by TradeDay, so I have no timings of my own; see our payout speed guide for firms where I do.

Pros, cons and the catches

Pros

  • Low monthly price and no activation fee
  • No daily loss limit; drawdown is the headline rule
  • Fast Pass uses end-of-day trailing, in the evaluation and funded
  • Trade copiers allowed (Tradesyncer listed)
  • Trustpilot 4.6 from 1,453 reviews (5 November 2026)

Cons

  • I haven't traded it and it hasn't paid me
  • Quick Pay funded accounts trail intraday; no static option
  • 50/50 split below $4,000 and a $10,000 profit cap on Quick Pay; Fast Pass payouts capped
  • Non-refundable subscription; sale prices may change
  • Payout and offboarding allegations; not SEC/CFTC registered
  • Day trading only, news blackout, strict banned-practice list
Watch out: TradeDay is not registered with the SEC or CFTC (its own footer says so), and accounts are simulated. Some Trustpilot reviewers allege funded accounts were disabled after a payout request. TradeDay replied that its approval rate is over 98% and that denials are for deliberate abuse of prohibited practices. I can't verify either side.
  • Payout and offboarding allegations. A 1-star review (14 August 2026) says three funded accounts were disabled after a first payout request over an alleged third-party service the reviewer says they didn't use. Another (26 May 2026) says a trader who withdrew about $9.8K in three days was offboarded; TradeDay said offboarding follows a risk review after a breach. These are allegations; TradeDay's rules say frequent large withdrawals near the drawdown may count as "mismanaging".
  • Subscription billing. One 1-star reviewer (21 September 2026) says a subscription kept billing after cancellation and a refund was refused; TradeDay said it would review.
  • Rules change. The 29 May 2026 relaunch replaced the old line-up and on 26 July 2026 Fast Pass caps changed. A 1-star reviewer (17 June 2026) says an account ended on a drawdown the dashboard didn't show live; TradeDay said traders monitor their own accounts.
  • Countries. Citizens or residents of about 80 countries can't open accounts, and Germany and Canada outside Ontario get Funded Sim only. Check TradeDay's list before you pay.
  • Bias. The two longest third-party reviews I read (DamnPropFirms, PropNavi) earn commission from TradeDay, so I used TradeDay's own pages.

Who it's for, and who should skip it

A possible fit:

  • Futures day traders who want a low monthly cost and pick the route by its funded drawdown (Fast Pass, if you only buy end-of-day).
  • Hand traders or copier users happy to trade small at first.

Pick another firm if you want end-of-day or static drawdown guaranteed on a big funded account, hold through the close or the news, run a bot you bought, can't afford a non-refundable month, or live in an excluded country. For a futures firm that has paid me, see Lucid Trading.

FAQ

Does TradeDay actually pay out?

I don't know first-hand: I haven't traded it and it hasn't paid me. TradeDay shows 4.6 on Trustpilot from 1,453 reviews and says over 98% of payouts are approved, but some reviewers allege accounts were disabled after payout requests. Those are unverified allegations, so test with a small account and an early payout.

Is TradeDay's drawdown trailing or end-of-day?

Both exist. Fast Pass is end-of-day trailing in the evaluation and when funded. Quick Pay comes as Intraday or EOD, but funded Quick Pay accounts calculate trailing drawdown intraday. There is no static option on the current plans.

What happens to profit above $10,000 on Quick Pay?

Per TradeDay's help center, each funded Quick Pay account is reviewed at $10,000 gross profit and profit above that is removed, not paid or transferred. Most traders then move to Funded Live with the carried-over balance. Taking payouts along the way avoids sitting on profit.

Is there a TradeDay discount code?

I don't have a code of my own, so I'm not listing one. TradeDay runs its own site-wide sale and the prices here are the ones it displayed on 5 November 2026. It may change, so check the checkout.

Does TradeDay have an activation fee, and can I get a refund?

There is no activation fee. Fees are non-refundable under the Terms. A reset is free if you wait for the next subscription payment; the cards show the reset at the monthly price but the Terms say $99, so check the checkout.
