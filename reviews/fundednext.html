The rules that end accounts

Most CFD firms have the same headline numbers. What ends accounts is the fine print. At FundedNext, these are the ones to read twice. I'd also read the help-center article for your exact plan before paying, because FundedNext has changed Stellar rules more than once this year (refunds on 12 January 2026, the Lite cycle on 12 October 2026).

1. The daily loss limit and the equity-based max loss

On the 2-Step, the daily limit is 5% of your initial balance: you must not lose more than that in a single trading day. FundedNext words the 10% max loss differently: your balance or equity must not go below 90% of the start. The word equity is the trap. It means an open trade that floats deep into the red can breach the account, even if it would have come back.

Example (my arithmetic, not a FundedNext case): on a 100K 2-Step the daily limit is $5,000 and the static floor is $90,000. Say a losing run has left you at $92,000 and you open a big gold trade. It floats $2,500 against you before it turns. Equity touched $89,500, under the floor, so the account is gone even though the trade was heading back to profit. Because the floor is static, it never rises as you profit. But open risk matters as much as closed losses. You could size so that your stop-out sits well inside half the daily limit.

The article doesn't say how the daily limit is measured or when the day resets, so check your plan's article before you rely on either.

2. Quick Strike: trades closed in under 30 seconds

On the 2-Step, FundedNext's help article says trades closed in under 30 seconds ("Quick Strike") must stay under 30%. This one is aimed at scalpers and tick-scalping approaches. If that's your style, read the article for exactly what's measured before you buy.

Example (assuming it is measured as a share of your profit; confirm with the article): you make $8,000 in profit, and $3,000 of it comes from trades held under 30 seconds. That's 37.5%, over the 30% line. A trader who scalps in and out gets caught without ever having a bad day.

This rule shows up in complaints. In the first page of recent Trustpilot one-star reviews (23 October to 4 November 2026) I counted four terminations blamed on "tick scalping" or copy trading, and one trader said a $200K Stellar 1-Step was terminated retroactively over the Quick Strike rule. Those are single users' unverified claims and I can't judge them. But a rule that triggers repeated complaints is one I'd treat as live.

3. The news rule (funded accounts)

This is a funded-account profit rule, not a challenge rule. Only 40% of profits from trades placed within 5 minutes before or after high-impact news on a correlated pair count toward the account. Losses stay fully with you.

Example: you make $1,000 on an NFP trade in that window. For profit purposes it counts as $400, while a $1,000 loss would count as a full $1,000. If you trade news on a funded account, plan for it. If you don't, you can ignore it. (On FundedNext Futures the rules are different: news is allowed there.)

Other rules to know

  • EAs and bots: allowed on Stellar below $50K if aligned with the account parameters, not allowed at $50K and above. I wouldn't assume they're allowed. See firms that allow bots.
  • Copy trading: it shows up in the termination complaints above. Don't copy between accounts without reading the rule for your plan.
  • Drawdown type: static on every challenge. If you want the difference explained, read trailing vs end-of-day drawdown.
  • Not stated in the articles I read: weekend and overnight rules and consistency rules for the challenges. Check before you rely on them.
