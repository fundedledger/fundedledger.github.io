My experience with Earn2Trade

I haven't traded Earn2Trade, and it hasn't paid me. Everything here comes from Earn2Trade's website and help center and from public reviews, all checked on 27 October 2026. I have no pass, no payout certificate and no experience of its support to show you.

What I can tell you is the rule I use for futures. After intraday trailing drawdown cost me Apex accounts again and again, I only buy end-of-day trailing. Even that has ended funded accounts of mine that were in profit. Earn2Trade's evaluation is end-of-day, but its Live accounts are trailing, so my rule only covers part of the journey here.

For comparison, Lucid Trading has paid me, and I can say so. Earn2Trade has not.

Drawdown: the nuance that matters

Earn2Trade calls its evaluation drawdown end-of-day, and that part is true: the floor rises $1 for every $1 of end-of-day gain, is adjusted only after the close, and stops at the starting balance. But the help center also says open equity losses are "taken into consideration when calculating whether or not the account failed". So a deep unrealised loss during the day can fail you even if you would have recovered by the close.

  • Evaluation and LiveSim: end-of-day drawdown, with open equity counted in real time.
  • Daily loss limit: tracked from 5pm to 5pm CT, includes open P&L and commissions. Breaching it fails the evaluation (a reset is needed), not just the day.
  • Live accounts: trailing drawdown, according to the programme pages. The $200K Live account is fixed instead.

Read that against my own rule: end-of-day trailing only on futures. The evaluation fits it. Live does not. If you pass, check exactly how the drawdown behaves before you rely on the funded stage. See trailing vs end-of-day drawdown for how the types differ.

Watch out

Watch out: Earn2Trade's own figure is that 8.89% of subscriptions passed its Trader Career Path and Gauntlet Mini examinations in 2025 (by Earn2Trade's own count of subscriptions passed against new subscriptions). Evaluations are recurring subscriptions with no refunds, including for automatic renewals. Trustpilot 1-star reviewers complain about the open-equity and daily-loss rules, accounts closed with no refund, and slow support. Trustpilot doesn't verify these claims, and 1-star reviews are about 7% of the total, but they point at the same rules described here.
  • Open-equity breach and a hard daily loss limit. $550 on TCP25 and $1,100 on a 50K account. See the drawdown section.
  • Recurring subscription, no refunds. Every month you don't pass costs the full monthly price again.
  • Funded-stage fees. A one-time $139 LiveSim activation fee per account (non-professional CME data status) is deducted from your first withdrawal. Professional status costs $140 a month per exchange in data fees. Live accounts carry data fees of $140 a month per exchange on Rithmic or $156 on NinjaTrader.
  • Payout provider fees. Rise charges $50 per withdrawal outside the US and 1.5% for US traders; Deel charges $50; Bayzat $5 to $40; direct crypto 0.735%. Your first payout needs to clear the $139 activation fee as well as these.
  • 50% or 80%, by withdrawal size. The split depends on how much you withdraw in one go, not on accumulated profit. Small withdrawals pay 50%.
  • LiveSim withdrawal caps. Total withdrawals from LiveSim are capped before you move to Live: $1,750 on TCP25, $3,000 on TCP50, $6,000 on TCP100 and $5,000 on every Gauntlet Mini size.
  • Not a regulated financial firm. Earn2Trade describes itself as an educational company and says it needs no financial licence. Funded stages run through a separate proprietary trading firm. I couldn't confirm that firm's name, or how long Earn2Trade has been operating, from official pages.
  • Bots and copy trading. I found no official rule, and third-party sources disagree. Ask support before you automate or copy anything.
  • Trustpilot. 4.5 from 5,046 reviews on 27 October 2026. Complaints include contract-limit failures, accounts closed before the maximum drawdown with no refund, and templated support replies.

Who Earn2Trade is for

A possible fit: discretionary futures traders who size trades so open losses stay well inside the daily loss limit, can pass within a month or two, and are fine withdrawing in larger chunks to reach the 80% split.

Not a good fit: scalpers who run deep intraday drawdowns, anyone who needs a refund option, and anyone who wants end-of-day trailing on the funded account too. If you want a firm that has paid me, look at Lucid Trading.
