The rules that end FTMO accounts

FTMO's rules are clearer than most. Three of them are where I'd expect people to lose accounts, so here they are in detail.

1. The daily loss limit

On the 2-Step the limit is 5% of your initial capital, recalculated at 00:00 Central European time, and it applies in both phases and on the funded account. FTMO's objectives page says the limit is measured from your balance at the start of that day. What I couldn't find on any FTMO page I read is the exact formula for floating losses. FTMO defines equity as balance plus unrealised profit and loss, but doesn't say in those words that the daily limit uses equity.

So here's how I'd treat it: assume open losses count, and check FTMO's Maximum Daily Loss section before relying on anything else.

Example, 100K 2-Step: you start the day at a $100,000 balance, so the day's floor is $95,000. You close a trade for -$3,000, and you're holding a second position that's $2,000 underwater. If floating losses count, you're at $95,000 and the account is gone, even if that position would have come back. Sizing so that a full stop-out of everything open can't cross the line is the habit that protects you. On the 1-Step the same example is tighter: the limit is $3,000, not $5,000.

2. The 1-Step's trailing max loss and the new best-day rule

The 1-Step's 10% max loss trails off your highest end-of-day balance. It can rise but never fall. By my reading of the page, if you finish a day at $104,000 on a 100K account, your floor becomes $94,000. After a good run you carry less room than a static floor would give you, which is why I prefer the 2-Step.

The second change is the best-day rule. For 1-Step orders created on or after 12 October 2026 (09:00 MT time), your best day must be no more than 50% of your total profit. Earlier orders use the older version, which measured against positive days' profit. FTMO says you can't breach this rule: you just keep trading until it's met. FTMO's own trading update of 8 October 2026 says the same.

Example: you reach the $10,000 target, but one day made $6,000. That day is 60% of the total, which is too much. To bring it under 50% you need total profit of at least $12,000 ($6,000 ÷ 0.5). So you'd have to keep trading, with the daily and max loss limits still live, for another $2,000. That's pure arithmetic, but it's where a good run turns into a risky one.

3. News and holding limits on the funded account

This is the catch I'd flag to anyone who holds trades over the weekend. In the Challenge and Verification, there's no news restriction and no break-holding restriction. On a funded Standard account, execution is restricted for 2 minutes before and after selected news, on symbols with the affected currency. You also have to close positions before any market break longer than 2 hours.

If you passed the challenge swinging trades through weekends, your funded routine has to change. The Swing account avoids both restrictions, but it's 2-Step only, and leverage drops from 1:100 to 1:30. You can switch Swing to Standard only if you have no trades, and not the other way round.

Bots and other rules

FTMO says it has no reason to limit EAs or algorithmic trading and doesn't require a stop-loss. There are limits to know about: 200 orders open at once, 2,000 positions per day, and a $400,000 total allocation cap across all your accounts (copy-trading from the same source counts). I'd also take FTMO's warning seriously that non-serious trading practices can end an account.

Getting paid

You can request your first reward on the 14th day after your first trade on the account, or any day after, once everything is closed. You also have to finish FTMO Identity (KYC) first. In my experience each payout then took about 2–3 days to process, and my four 2026 payouts ranged from $4,604 to $9,265.

  • Split: 80% on the 2-Step, 90% through the Scaling Plan or Premium Programme. The 1-Step and Instant pay 90% from the start.
  • Example, 100K 2-Step: $5,000 of closed profit pays $4,000 at 80%, or $4,500 at 90%.
  • Minimums: $20 closed profit for bank wire and Visa Direct or Mastercard Send, $50 for crypto.
  • Methods: bank wire, Visa Direct / Mastercard Send (up to $20,000), Skrill (up to $3,000) and crypto. FTMO charges no extra withdrawal fee.
  • Speed per FTMO: it replies in 1-2 business days and typically sends the reward 1-2 business days after the invoice is approved.
  • After the first reward: some aggregator sites mention a bi-weekly cycle, but FTMO's FAQ states no fixed cycle, so I won't promise one.

For the full step-by-step with dated FTMO sources, read how FTMO payouts work.

Scaling: the Scaling Plan adds 25% to your account size every 4 months, up to $2,000,000 across accounts. You need 4 months trading, at least 10% net profit above the starting balance in that window, at least 2 processed rewards, and a positive balance. A $100K account becomes $125K on those terms. Keeping the 90% split is valid for the 2-Step only.

The catches I'd keep in mind:

Funded isn't the same as challenge. News and break-holding limits only start on the funded Standard account, so a style that passed the challenge can break a funded account.

The refund is slow. Your fee returns only once you request your first reward, so it's not a discount on day one.

Support can be slow. Trustpilot reviewers praise fast payouts but criticise slow or unanswered support emails and sudden account restrictions. Those are user claims.

The rules change. The 1-Step best-day rule changed on 12 October 2026. Check the objectives page for the order you're buying, because older orders keep the older rule.

FAQ

Does FTMO actually pay out?

Yes. It has paid me four times in 2026, $26,138.16 in total, and each payout took about 2-3 days to process. FTMO itself says rewards are typically sent within 1-2 business days after the invoice is approved. My certificates are on this page, with my name blurred.

Should I buy the 1-Step or the 2-Step?

For most traders I would pick the 2-Step. Its max loss is a fixed 10% floor, the daily loss limit is 5% instead of 3%, and the fee is refunded after your first reward request. The 1-Step pays 90% from the start, but its max loss trails the highest end-of-day balance, its daily limit is tighter, and since 12 October 2026 it has a best-day rule that can stretch the challenge.

Is the FTMO challenge fee refunded?

On the 2-Step, according to FTMO's FAQ, the fee is refunded to your original payment method when you make your first Reward withdrawal request. The 1-Step and Instant fees are not refundable. Fees from failed attempts are not refunded.

Can I hold trades over the weekend at FTMO?

In the Challenge and Verification, yes. On a funded Standard account you must close positions before any market break longer than 2 hours, which includes weekends. The Swing account removes that restriction and the news restriction, but it is 2-Step only and leverage drops to 1:30.

Can I use EAs or bots at FTMO?

FTMO says it has no reason to limit discretionary, algorithmic or EA trading, and no stop-loss is required. Watch the limits: 200 orders at a time, 2,000 positions per day, a $400,000 total allocation cap across your accounts, and a warning that non-serious trading practices can end an account.

Is there an FTMO discount code?

Not from us. I have no code to give you, so I will not invent one. FTMO runs its own sales from time to time, so check its pricing page before you buy.

How long does it take to get paid after passing?

You can request your first reward on the 14th day after your first trade on the account, or any day after, once all positions are closed. You must also finish FTMO Identity verification (KYC). FTMO's FAQ does not state a payout cycle after the first reward, so check that before you plan around it.
