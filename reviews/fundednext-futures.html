The rules that matter, explained

FundedNext has a long rules list. Three of them are the ones I'd expect to end accounts, so I'll go through those properly and then list the rest briefly.

1. The drawdown is end-of-day, but the check is live

Every current plan uses end-of-day trailing drawdown, not intraday trailing. The max loss level follows your highest end-of-day balance and never moves down after losses. That's the type I buy on futures.

Here's the part that's easy to miss. FundedNext's help center says that if your floating loss reaches the max loss limit during an active trade, the account is breached instantly, even if the trade would have come back. The level only moves at the end of the day; the check runs on live equity all day. Whether floating profit moves the level during the day is not stated anywhere I read. See trailing vs end-of-day drawdown for the general idea.

Example (my arithmetic, not a FundedNext worked example): Flex 50K starts at $50,000 with a $1,500 max loss, so the first floor is $48,500. Say you close a day at $51,000. The floor moves to $49,500. The next day you take a trade that floats the account down to $49,500 before it turns around. On the help center's wording, that's a breach, even if you'd have closed in profit. On NQ, $1,500 is 75 points on one contract. The floor stops trailing at the starting balance plus $100 ($50,100 on a 50K), which you reach once an end-of-day balance hits $51,600. The Flex page and the trading-objectives page say it locks at the initial balance instead, so I say "about the starting balance".

2. The Flex 40% consistency rule (challenge only)

In the Flex challenge, no single day should be more than 40% of the profit target. If you go over, the challenge doesn't fail. The bar goes up: your required total profit becomes your best day divided by 0.40. It applies to the challenge only, and the funded Flex account has no consistency rule.

Example (my arithmetic): Flex 50K has a $2,500 target, so the best-day cap is $1,000. If you have a $1,400 day, you now need $1,400 / 0.40 = $3,500 in total profit, which is $1,000 more than the plain target. There's no time limit, so it costs time and exposure, not the account.

3. No overnight holds, and the 10-second scalping rule

Everything has to be flat by 3:10 PM CT, Monday to Friday. Positions still open are auto-closed. Trading resumes at 5:00 PM CT. If you're used to leaving a runner on, this is the rule that would catch you.

Separately, trades closed within 10 seconds trigger a warning at 30% of profit, and at 40% the profit from them is deducted for the cycle. The help article says the account isn't terminated.

The rest, briefly

  • Daily loss: none on Flex and Rapid Pro. Rapid Daily and Direct have one, but it's a soft breach: trading pauses until the 5:00 PM CT reset.
  • Inactivity: 30 consecutive calendar days without a trade breaches the challenge or deactivates the funded account. On Direct, you need at least one trade a week.
  • Contracts (Flex): 3 minis or 30 micros on 50K, 5 / 50 on 100K, 8 / 80 on 150K. News trading is allowed on Flex; bots are allowed through a platform integration with no FundedNext support.
  • Platforms and access: NinjaTrader and Tradovate (Direct also lists TradingView). The US isn't on the restricted-country list; many other countries are.

The Rapid Pro catch: no buffer rule, but the floor doesn't move

FundedNext says Rapid Pro has no buffer rule, and that's true: it's in the Futures help center. Rapid Daily has a buffer (above), and Rapid Pro doesn't. What I didn't take from that wording is what happens to your room to lose after a payout. The loss floor doesn't move when you withdraw, so the money you take out comes straight out of your cushion.

My own numbers, on a Rapid Pro 25K: my balance was $26,094 and the floor was locked at $25,100, so I had $994 of room. I requested $800. After the request my balance was $25,294, which leaves $194 of room. The next payout then needs $500 of profit in the cycle. The math: room after payout = balance − payout − floor, so $26,094 − $800 − $25,100 = $194.

To be clear, all of this is documented, and I'm not calling it a scam: the payout did arrive. It's the wording that felt misleading to me, because "no buffer" reads as "nothing holds your payout back", while in practice a withdrawal can leave you a day's bad trading away from a breach. That's my opinion, so check the rules yourself. Before you request, work out your room after the payout with the formula above, and ask yourself whether you'd rather wait and request a smaller amount.
