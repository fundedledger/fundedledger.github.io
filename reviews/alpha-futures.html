My experience with Alpha Futures

I haven't traded Alpha Futures, and it hasn't paid me. I have no pass, no payout certificate and no experience of its support to show you. Everything here comes from the firm's own website and help center, plus public reviews, checked on November 4, 2026.

What I can offer is how I judge a futures firm after 100+ evaluations bought and 40+ passed (I've failed more than I've passed). Intraday trailing drawdown cost me Apex accounts again and again, so I only buy end-of-day trailing on futures. Alpha Futures says none of its accounts use intraday trailing, which is why I looked at it. Even end-of-day trailing has ended funded accounts of mine that were in profit, so don't read "end-of-day" as "forgiving".

For comparison, Lucid Trading has paid me, and I can say so. Alpha Futures has not.

Watch out: July 2026 and payout complaints

New NinjaTrader/Tradovate accounts are no longer available, and the firm's own platform page no longer lists NinjaTrader. The Premium plan was discontinued, and outlets also report its accounts were closed; they differ on how Premium customers were refunded. Four of the newest one-star Trustpilot reviews (31 October to 4 November 2026) say Premium payouts are pending since June or July. Trustpilot doesn't verify these claims.

  • Payouts allegedly withheld after termination. Two recent reviewers say approved payouts were withheld after their accounts were terminated, one alleging about $59,000 and one about $42,000. I can't verify either.

The rules that matter

I won't list every rule here. These are the ones I'd watch because they end accounts or block payouts.

1. Drawdown: end-of-day trailing, but checked live

The max loss limit trails your highest end-of-day balance and stops trailing at your starting balance. Per the firm's own article, a 50K Zero account starts at a max loss limit of $48,000; an end-of-day gain of $500 moves it to $48,500, and a later loss doesn't lower it. That's the end-of-day behaviour I look for.

The catch is that the breach check isn't end-of-day. The firm says: "If you break MLL at any point, floating equity or closed balance of account, your account will be liquidated." So a trade that dips below the line during the session can end the account even if you'd have closed back above it. How floating profit affects the trailing level isn't stated, so I won't guess. A breached Qualified account is closed; a breached evaluation needs a reset or the next rebill.

2. Consistency: the rule that blocks a pass or a payout

Consistency means one day can't be too big a share of your net profit. In evaluations it's 50% on Standard, 40% on Advanced and none on Zero, and you don't pass until it's satisfied. Once Qualified it's 40% on Zero and Standard, 20% on Direct and none on Advanced, measured on net profit since your last withdrawal request. In Qualified it blocks a withdrawal rather than breaching the account.

Example (labeled, from the firm's own figures): on a Standard 50K evaluation with a $3,000 target and the 50% rule, your best day can't exceed $1,500 of the net profit. If you hit $3,000 with one $2,000 day, you haven't passed yet; you need more profit from other days until that day is 50% or less. On Advanced 50K the 40% rule gives a largest allowed day of $1,600.

3. News blackout on funded Zero, Standard and Direct

Evaluations and Advanced Qualified have no news restriction. On Zero, Standard and Direct Qualified accounts you can't place orders within 2 minutes before or after ForexFactory red-folder news (4 minutes for speeches; the firm's article says 2 minutes in one place and 4 in another). Holding a position through the news is fine. A first violation is a warning, and the trade's profit is voided and the payout denied; later violations breach the account.

The other rules in brief

  • Bots: "AI, bots, and other automated trading mechanisms is strictly prohibited across all account types", and 100+ trades a day is restricted.
  • Also banned: hedging, reverse trading, account rolling or stacking, group trading, order-book spamming, tight-bracket exploits, and a VPN to hide your IP. The penalty is immediate termination with profits voided.
  • Daily Loss Guard: a soft breach. On Zero it's 2% of the starting balance ($500 / $1,000 / $2,000); at that open-plus-closed loss, positions are flattened and you're locked out until 6PM ET the next day. The account survives. The pricing table shows Standard Qualified limits ($1,000 / $2,000 / $3,000) that the help article doesn't cover.

Payouts

  • Split: you receive 90% of each request.
  • Frequency: you can request after every 5 winning days of $200 or more (they don't need to be consecutive), up to 4 requests a month.
  • Size: up to 50% of account profit per request, capped by plan.
  • Minimums: Zero $200, Standard $500, Direct $500, Advanced $1,000.
  • Caps: Zero $1,000 / $1,500 / $2,500 (25K / 50K / 100K); Standard $3,000 / $4,000 / $5,000 (50K / 100K / 150K); Advanced $15,000 on every size; Direct $1,000 / $2,000 / $2,500 / $3,000 (25K to 150K). The plan table says "Max Payout $15,000" for Advanced, but the homepage also says "up to $15,000 in performance fees per cycle", and the payout article words the other caps per request. Sources differ on whether the Advanced figure is per request or per cycle, so confirm before you count on it.
  • Speed: conflicting, see the Watch out section.

Example (labeled, using the firm's rules): a Zero 50K Qualified account sitting on $4,000 of profit. 50% of that is $2,000, but the plan cap is $1,500, so the request is $1,500 and you receive 90%, which is $1,350.

What it really costs

List price is the small part. With 100+ evaluations bought, I plan for more than one try. To model the full bill with failed attempts, resets and fees, use our prop firm cost calculator.

Evaluations rebill on the same day each month until you pass or cancel, and the dashboard prompts you to reset or continue 24 hours before the rebill. A failed evaluation restores the starting balance at the next rebill, or you can buy a paid reset without changing your rebill date. There's no stated evaluation time limit; the monthly rebill is the clock.

Examples (labeled, arithmetic on the list prices above, not a prediction):

  • Standard 50K, three months to pass: 3 × $129 = $387. Add one $109 reset and it's $496.
  • Zero 50K, three months to pass: 3 × $169 = $507. Add one $149 reset and it's $656 ($566 at the $139 price some sources show).

Cost traps: Qualified resets cost $399 to $999 on Zero and Standard (see Cons). The subscription keeps rebilling until you cancel.

FAQ

Does Alpha Futures actually pay out?

I don't know first-hand: I haven't traded it and it hasn't paid me. The firm publishes payout rules and says it has paid $70M in performance fees (its own, unaudited figure). Trustpilot reviewers also allege long delays, mostly on the discontinued Premium plan, and payouts withheld after account termination. Those are unverified claims from individual users. Test with a small first payout.

How fast are Alpha Futures payouts?

Sources differ. The homepage banner says you receive it the same day. The homepage FAQ says approved payouts are currently paid within five business days or less. The payout policy article states no timeframe. Some Trustpilot reviewers report much longer waits. Plan on the five-business-day figure at best.

Is Alpha Futures drawdown trailing?

Yes, but end-of-day trailing, not intraday. The firm says none of its accounts use intraday trailing. The level follows your highest end-of-day balance and stops trailing at your starting balance. A breach is still checked on live equity, so a floating loss can end the account.

Can I use NinjaTrader or Tradovate with Alpha Futures?

Not for new accounts. Third-party news outlets report that the NinjaTrader partnership ended on 12 July 2026, and the firm's own platform page no longer lists it. You can use AlphaTrader, TradingView (powered by Plus500), WealthCharts, DeepChart or Quantower.

Can I use a trading bot or EA at Alpha Futures?

No. The firm's prohibited-practices article says AI, bots and other automated trading are strictly prohibited across all account types. Semi-automation is allowed if you place and manage the trades yourself.

Is there an Alpha Futures promo code or an activation fee?

I don't have a code of my own to give you, so I'm not listing one. There is no activation fee on accounts bought after the 8 July 2026 announcement. Older Advanced Qualified accounts were charged $149.

Can I hold trades overnight at Alpha Futures?

The firm's trading-hours article says all trades must be closed before 4:20PM EST every day. It doesn't use the words overnight or weekend, but that rule means positions can't be held past that time.
