The rules that matter

Per FXIFY's blog and FAQ, the trailing max loss follows your highest closed balance and static is fixed at the starting balance. The daily loss is measured from the previous day's balance at 5 PM EST and judged on real-time equity, so open losses count live. A trailing floor locks permanently once profit reaches the drawdown percentage or a payout is processed. The table is from FXIFY's pages, checked 5 November 2026.

Three rules decide most outcomes here, in my reading.

1. Which drawdown your plan has

This is the big one, and it's why I'd treat the plan choice as the real decision. Here's a worked example on a Standard $100K account (example numbers, my arithmetic). Max loss is 10%, or $10,000, so the floor starts at $90,000 and rises with your highest closed balance. You close up $4,000 (balance $104,000), so the floor is now $94,000. An open trade then floats $6,500 against you and equity drops to $97,500: still above the floor. But the daily limit is 4%, or $4,000, measured on live equity from the previous 5 PM EST balance. A $4,000 floating drop on that day would hit the daily limit well before the max-loss floor. The floor only locks at $100,000 once closed profit reaches $10,000 or a payout is processed.

Compare Classic $100K: the floor sits at $90,000 whatever you do, and the daily loss is $4,000. One-line version: on static plans you know where the line is every day; on trailing plans it moves with your closed profit.

2. The daily loss, especially on One Phase

One Phase is the tightest set: 3% daily and 6% trailing. On a $100K account (example) that is $3,000 a day: if you are up $5,000 and give back $3,000, you breach the daily rule alone, even in profit overall.

3. Plan-specific restrictions

Pro has the $4,000 daily profit cap once funded (the account goes read-only for the day) and the +0.5% rule for a day to count. Classic's funded stage has a 25% consistency rule; the exact formula isn't on the pages I read, so check it before you rely on it. Example only: with a 25% limit and $8,000 of total profit, no single day should be above $2,000.

Other rules, briefly: a hard breach if you place no trade in 60 days; EAs, martingale and grid are allowed per the homepage, but third parties report no EAs on Instant Funding or Lightning, and some reviewers report EAs flagged at withdrawal. FXIFY's blog says skip Lightning and both Instant tiers if you trade news. Copy trading is not allowed on Instant, Lightning or Pro (unless Pro is the master). See trailing vs end-of-day drawdown for how the types differ.

Where FXIFY's pages disagree: the homepage says there's no minimum trading days; program pages say 5; the Pro post says 3. The homepage is marketing, so I'd plan for the per-program number. The older Two Phase wording ("10% trailing, 10% and 5% targets") is the Standard plan; Classic and Pro are the 2026 versions. A December 2023 blog post showing different One Phase numbers is out of date.

Payouts

  • First payout: on demand on One, Two and Three Phase, "as soon as the first trade on the live account is closed". Minimum $50.
  • After that: once a month, or every two weeks with the bi-weekly add-on. Pro pays out every 10 days; Instant Funding every 14 days; Lightning after 7 days, then every 14; Classic every 14 or 30 days.
  • Caps: the homepage says there are no limits on withdrawals, but the Pro post caps the first two withdrawals at "5% of the initial balance or $8,000" (I'd quote it as given; the wording is ambiguous). From the third on, there's no cap.
  • Split: the headline is "up to 90%", and that needs a paid "extra 15%" add-on. The base split isn't published. Classic's blog says 80% on a 14-day cycle or 100% on a 30-day cycle, Pro is 80/20 with no add-on, and the homepage hero says "up to 100%". With that many numbers, I wouldn't count on more than what the checkout shows.
  • Speed: FXIFY's pages say "instant" for the first payout but give no hours or days. A third-party review (QuantVPS, 29 October 2026) says requests are reviewed Monday to Friday, 9am to 9pm GMT, in 2 to 3 business days, and that the first one needs agreements signed through Riseworks.
  • Add-ons and scaling: add-on prices aren't published, and I found no scaling plan on any official page.

What traders complain about

Trustpilot shows 4.3 out of 5 from 6,597 reviews as of 5 November 2026 (2,287 in the last 12 months). 78% are five-star and 12% are one-star. FXIFY replies to 96% of negative reviews, usually within 24 hours, and the page shows it invites reviews. These reviews are unverified and company-invited, so everything below is "reviewers say".

The 1-star pile is dominated by one issue:

  • Payouts denied or forfeited. Reviewers say payouts were cancelled for alleged latency arbitrage or prohibited trading without evidence. Examples dated October 2026 include a $2,400 payout cancelled, $2,321.86 forfeited after five days "under review", a $4,000 third payout denied with the account banned, and a fourth payout that led to closure after three were paid. FXIFY's reply is that it can't disclose detection methods and that decisions are final.
  • Slow payouts and disputed breaches. Some payouts took more than 10 business days, and reviewers dispute breaches such as a failed mobile-app close. Others describe bans after disputes; FXIFY says the cause was abusive conduct towards staff.

The praise is real too: fast chat and email support, accounts delivered within minutes of purchase, "clear" rules, and several reviewers saying they were paid a number of times over six months. A competitor review site (MyForexFirms, July 2026) also alleges a $4,310 denial and an EA flagged at withdrawal; I'd treat that as another allegation, from a source that competes with FXIFY.

My read: most reviews are positive and many people report being paid, but the 1-star complaints cluster on payouts. I found no regulator warning, though I did not search the registers. I can't verify any single story, and I won't claim a pass or payout rate. Reviewers say payouts were denied under latency-arbitrage or prohibited-trading rules, and I can't tell you what those traders actually did. If you scalp very fast, read FXIFY's prohibited-strategies rules before buying, and keep your first account small.

FAQ

Does FXIFY actually pay out?

I don't know first-hand: I haven't traded it and it hasn't paid me. Trustpilot shows 4.3 from 6,597 reviews (5 November 2026), and many reviewers say they were paid. But the 1-star reviews cluster on payouts denied or forfeited under latency-arbitrage or prohibited-trading rules. These reviews are unverified and company-invited, so test with a small account and request an early payout.

Is FXIFY's drawdown static or trailing?

It depends on the plan. Classic, Pro and Three Phase are static. Standard, One Phase, Instant Funding and Lightning are trailing, following your highest closed balance. Daily loss is judged on real-time equity on all of them.

Is there an FXIFY discount code?

We don't have one, and I'm not printing FXIFY's own sale codes because they expire quickly. FXIFY runs sitewide sales; a 25% November sale was running when I checked on 5 November. Look at the checkout the day you buy.

How much does FXIFY cost?

FXIFY doesn't publish a price table on its program pages. Only Two Phase Pro is official, from $129 for $10K to $1,350 for $250K. Third-party trackers list Two Phase Classic and Standard from about $59 ($5K) to $549 ($100K). Check the checkout for the real price.

Does FXIFY refund the fee?

FXIFY says the fee is reimbursed with your first payout, on request, for One, Two and Three Phase. It isn't stated for Lightning or Instant, and a third-party source says Two Phase Pro fees are not refundable. The refund only comes if you reach a payout.

Can I use an EA or trade news on FXIFY?

FXIFY's homepage says EAs, martingale and grid are allowed. Third parties report EAs are not allowed on Instant Funding or Lightning, and FXIFY's blog advises skipping those plans if you trade news. Some reviewers report EAs flagged at withdrawal, so check the current rules first.

Is FXIFY open to US residents?

No. The United States is on FXIFY's restricted list, along with Russia, Iran, Iraq, Cuba, Venezuela, Kenya, Ghana and others. Check the footer of FXIFY's site for the full list.
