Futures or CFD: which should you choose?

Pick the market you already trade. A firm's rules matter less than trading the instrument you know.

Futures firms

  • Trade regulated CME contracts (ES, NQ, CL, GC)
  • Rules set in dollars, e.g. $2,000 max loss on 50K
  • Usually trailing drawdown: learn end-of-day vs intraday
  • Often a single evaluation phase
  • Best for: US index and commodity day traders

CFD & forex firms

  • Trade forex pairs, indices and gold as CFDs
  • Rules set in percentages, e.g. 5% daily, 10% max
  • Often a fixed (non-trailing) max loss
  • Commonly two phases to pass
  • Best for: forex and swing traders, and traders outside the US

FAQ

Are prop firms legit?

The established ones pay out, but the business model depends on most traders failing and paying evaluation fees. Many funded accounts are simulated. Stick to firms with a long payout record, read the payout rules before you buy, and treat the fee as money you might lose.

What's the easiest prop firm challenge to pass?

Easiest to pass usually means no consistency rule, no minimum days and end-of-day drawdown. Apex and Alpha Futures' Zero plan fit that in 2026. But easy to pass doesn't mean easy to get paid: check the payout rules too.

How much does it really cost to get funded?

Multiply the fee by the number of attempts you realistically need, then add any activation fee. A $150 challenge passed on the third try costs about $450. Compare firms on that total.
