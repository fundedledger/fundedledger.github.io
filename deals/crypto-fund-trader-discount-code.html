The FINMA warning and Trustpilot

  • Swiss regulator warning. Crypto Fund Trader is reported to have been added to the FINMA warning list in August 2024. I haven't independently verified the date on FINMA's own page, which loads dynamically, so treat it as sourced from a third-party report. The firm's footer says its Swiss entity "is not authorized or licensed in Switzerland", and its homepage says "It's all demo, no real capital involved."
  • Where the company is based. Sources differ. The firm's footer names a Swiss AG in Zug. A third-party report describes a Spain-based company.
  • Trustpilot. The rating is hidden for a guideline breach and fake reviews were removed (checked 23 October 2026, not re-checked). I didn't know about either until our research found them.
  • Other traders' reports. One third-party report mentions denied payouts and a $10,000 per-day profit ceiling. The author reports no denials, and the $10,000 per-day ceiling is unconfirmed by us and comes from a single source. I report both sides because you should weigh both.

What a discount doesn't fix

  • The fee is one-time and not guaranteed back. I haven't read the refund or reset terms, so check them before paying. A lower fee doesn't change the odds of passing.
  • Passing takes more than one try. It took me about 3 accounts to get one funded. Budget for retries, not a single fee. Across all firms I pass roughly one in three.
  • The accounts are simulated. The firm says no real capital is involved. Payouts depend on the firm's willingness to pay, not on a regulated broker.
  • Rules still apply. Phase 1 has an 8% target, 5% daily loss and 10% overall loss; Phase 2 a 5% target with the same limits (1 Nov 2026 listing). Breaking a rule ends the account, whatever you paid.

Watch out

  • Regulatory red flag. The FINMA listing and the firm's own "not licensed" footer are real reasons to size your account small.
  • Hidden Trustpilot score. You can't use it to cross-check payout complaints.
  • Self-reported payouts. The homepage counter of money paid to traders is the firm's own number and can't be checked.
  • Minimum trading days. The site now lists none. My accounts needed five per phase. Sources differ, so read the current rules.
  • Promo wording. The Halloween promo is time-limited and shows no terms. Don't buy because of a countdown.
