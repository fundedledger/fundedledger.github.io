Alpha Futures vs Apex (2026)

A newer monthly-subscription firm that only uses end-of-day trailing, against the one-time-fee firm that lets you pick your drawdown. The rules differ more than the headline prices suggest.

Updated October 2026I've bought Apex; I haven't traded Alpha Futures

Disclosure: this page contains affiliate links. If you sign up through them I may earn a commission, at no extra cost to you. Right now only the Alpha Futures link earns us anything; the Apex link does not. I've tried to judge both on their rules, not on who pays.

Short answer: on drawdown, Alpha Futures is the safer design, because every account uses end-of-day trailing and there is no intraday option to pick by mistake. Apex offers end-of-day too, but also sells intraday trailing, and third-party sources report no resets and a funded account that closes after six payouts. Alpha has a thinner record, a July 2026 platform shake-up and payout-delay complaints. Neither is a clear winner.

My experience

I haven't traded Alpha Futures, and it hasn't paid me, so everything about it comes from its official site and help center. I've bought Apex evaluations, but Apex has not paid me. Intraday trailing drawdown cost me Apex accounts again and again, so I stopped buying it. On futures I only buy end-of-day trailing.

Sources differ on Apex. Apex's own site and help center returned an access error when I tried to read them on October 26, 2026. Every Apex number below comes from third-party reviews, mostly affiliate sites, and they disagree with each other. Apex also rebuilt its product in March 2026, so older reviews are wrong. Treat Apex figures as unverified and read Apex's checkout page before you buy.

Drawdown: the biggest difference

Alpha states that none of its accounts use intraday trailing drawdown. That removes the mistake I made at Apex. Apex reportedly lets you choose, and the intraday option counts unrealized profit when it moves the floor up, which is how a good trade that pulls back can end an account. Apex's end-of-day option avoids that. If you go with Apex, that's the one to pick, and it's chosen when you buy.

Costs: subscription vs one-time fee

Alpha bills monthly until you pass, so a slow evaluation gets more expensive, but there's no deadline and no activation fee on newer accounts. Resets cost $79 to $449 on evaluations and $399 to $999 on Qualified accounts. Apex is reported as a single fee with a 30-day clock, a possible activation fee after you pass, and no resets at all. Because Apex prices vary so much between sources and promotions, I won't quote one number. Compare Alpha's list price with Apex's live checkout price, including any activation fee, on the day you buy.

Payouts and consistency

Alpha pays 90%, allows up to four requests a month, and caps each request at 50% of your profit and at a plan limit. The caps are small on Zero ($1,000 to $2,500) and large on Advanced ($15,000), but Advanced has a higher target and, on 50K, a tighter drawdown. Apex's reported payout caps start low and rise on larger accounts, and the funded account closes after six payouts. Both firms apply a consistency check that blocks a withdrawal rather than breaking the account. If most of your profit comes from one big day, both will slow you down.

Where Alpha Futures wins

  • No intraday trailing anywhere. You can't choose the drawdown that cost me Apex accounts.
  • No evaluation deadline, and resets exist. Apex is reported to have neither.
  • Official, readable rules. Alpha's help center spells out the payout policy, drawdown and prohibited practices.

Watch out (Alpha Futures): it ended its NinjaTrader partnership on 12 July 2026, and its rules have changed repeatedly this year. Some Trustpilot reviewers report long payout delays; those claims are unverified, but test it with a small first payout. Bots and hedging are banned, there's a news blackout on three Qualified account types, and each payout request is capped at 50% of profit.

Where Apex wins

  • One-time fee and no evaluation consistency rule (as reported), so one strong stretch can finish the evaluation.
  • More platform choice. Rithmic, Tradovate and NinjaTrader are reported as available, which Alpha no longer offers to new accounts.
  • An end-of-day option. If you choose it, you avoid the intraday trap.

Watch out (Apex): no resets, a 30-day clock, a six-payout limit, a possible activation fee, and an intraday option that cost me accounts. Prices are heavily coupon-driven, and I couldn't verify any rule against Apex's own pages.
