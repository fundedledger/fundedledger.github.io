Where the sources disagree

Sources differ on:
  • CFT minimum trading days. The FAQ says at least 5 different days in each phase unless you buy the add-on. The homepage cards say 0. I do not say “no minimum” because I can't confirm it. My own best case was about a week per phase, with 5 trading days as the minimum.
  • CFT Instant. The homepage still lists it, but the FAQ and the Terms say it is no longer for sale. I don't treat it as buyable.
  • CFT Break drawdown. The homepage says the limit “cannot be reached by equity”. The FAQ says breaches are calculated on equity.
  • CFT reverse trading. The FAQ allows opposite positions after 24 hours unless both are held 60 seconds or more; the Terms ban “hedging or reverse trading” outright. Follow the stricter wording.
  • CFT entity. The firm names a Swiss company and a Spanish one (next section).

Who is behind each firm

Crypto Fund Trader names SWISS RLCRATES AG, with an address in Zug, Switzerland, in its footer and Terms. Its own refund page says SWISS RLCRATES AG is not authorized or licensed in Switzerland, does not offer trading services with MT5 and does not conduct regulated activities. It describes itself as a payment and marketing agent for RLCRATES, S.L., which it names as the company that provides the MT5 service, with an office in Spain. So the firm's own pages name two entities. Neither is shown as a regulated broker, and CFT does not state a regulator on the pages we read. CFT's accounts are demo accounts: the homepage says “It's all demo, no real capital involved”, and payouts are called “scholarship rewards”.

FTMO does not state a regulator on the pages we read either. CFT sells simulated-account evaluations, not a regulated brokerage relationship, and we did not search regulator registers for either.

On the FINMA point: third-party sites report a warning on Crypto Fund Trader, but none we found links to a FINMA page, and FINMA's warning list is script-rendered, so we could not read its entries. We could not confirm it. The issuing body, the date and any response from CFT are unknown to us. If this matters to your decision, search FINMA's warning list yourself.

Drawdown: static or trailing

This is the rule that ends most accounts, so check the exact program before you buy.

  • 2-step programs are alike. CFT's 2-Phase has a static 10% maximum loss of the initial balance and a 5% daily limit measured from the 12:05 AM UTC balance. FTMO's 2-Step has a static 10% maximum loss and a 5% daily limit measured from the balance at 00:00 CE(S)T, and open profit and loss count toward it. Neither trails.
  • 1-step programs differ. CFT's Classic, Pro and Turbo are static, with tighter limits (6%, 5% and 3% maximum loss, 3% daily). FTMO's 1-Step is an end-of-day trailing 10% that can rise but never fall, and its Instant account trails intraday from the highest closed balance.
  • CFT's older 1-Phase (existing accounts only) was a balance-based trailing 6% that stops at the initial balance. New 1-Step accounts are static.

On CFD firms I only trade static drawdown. Trailing vs end-of-day drawdown explains the types.

Two clocks at CFT. Daily losses are measured from 12:05 AM UTC. The $10,000 daily profit cap resets at 12:10 AM UTC. Don't assume they share a rollover time. FTMO uses one clock, 00:00 CE(S)T, for the daily loss limit.

Fees, refunds and payouts

CFT's list prices on the pages we read are $389 for a 2-Phase 50K and $660 for a 100K, with 1-Step Turbo from $249 for 50K. We can't say which firm is cheaper, because we could not read an official FTMO price list, and third-party price pages disagree with each other. Check FTMO's pricing and compare the same account size at checkout.

Refunds work differently. FTMO refunds the 2-Step fee once you request your first Reward, to the same payment method; 1-Step and Instant fees are not refundable. CFT's refund page offers 14 days with no reason needed, but you lose it “if you have already opened a position on your demo account”, and the refund is at CFT's discretion. Its Terms also say you are not entitled to any diploma, reward or refund if you don't fully complete the phases.

Payout rules: CFT's first payout comes after 15 traded days, then every 30 calendar days on the standard path, and requires KYC with ID and proof of address. FTMO's first reward is available from the 14th day after your first trade, with all positions and pending orders closed. FTMO lists $20 and $50 minimums; I found no minimum or maximum stated for CFT.

Per the Terms, CFT “does not guarantee the payment of the paid scholarship” and may decline it despite you meeting the targets. That is the legal wording behind the “demo account” model.

Trust signals

The negatives: Trustpilot's sidebar says the company hasn't replied to negative reviews, and the newest 2-star review (10 November 2026) describes an outage on 20 October 2026 that stopped position closes, slow support, a max-loss breach the next day and a complaint still unresolved. That is one reviewer's account, which I haven't verified.

Crypto Fund Trader: Trustpilot shows “This company's rating is unavailable due to a breach of our guidelines” and says it has removed a number of fake reviews for this company. There are 1,113 reviews, 160 in the last 12 months: 77% 5 stars, 14% 1 star. Reviewers from September and October 2026 complain about payout denials, and the newest 1-star review (13 November 2026) describes dashboard problems on Bybit that forced the reviewer to calculate daily drawdown by hand. The company has replied to many reviews with templated text. My own experience is the opposite on payouts: about $15,000 paid with no denials. Both can be true. I can only speak to my own accounts, and in my experience they pay as long as I follow their rules, so read them closely.

Where Crypto Fund Trader wins

  • No time limit on evaluations. Published list prices start at $249 (1-Step Turbo 50K); I can't compare them with FTMO's, see above.
  • Crypto access. MT5 and Match-Trader with hundreds of crypto pairs, plus Bybit USDT futures, with weekend and overnight holds allowed per the FAQ.
  • Static drawdown across the 1-Step line as well as the 2-Phase.

Watch out (Crypto Fund Trader):

  • A $10,000 daily profit cap per day and/or per trade. On big accounts this limits what a good day counts for.
  • Hedging and reverse trading are banned in the Terms, even though the FAQ describes a 24-hour and 60-second rule.
  • Copy trading is banned on Ascend. Multiple accounts from the same household, device or IP/VPS are not allowed, and eligibility can be checked with KYC, payment, device and IP data.
  • The refund is forfeited once you open a position on the demo account.
  • The Trustpilot rating is suppressed and fake reviews have been removed.
  • Demo-only accounts, with payouts called “scholarship rewards” and no payment guarantee (Terms section 14.2).
  • The homepage and FAQ disagree on minimum days, Instant's sale status, leverage and instrument counts.
  • An entity that disclaims licensing in Switzerland, and an unconfirmed third-party FINMA claim (see above).

Where FTMO wins

  • Clearer published rules. The trading objectives page states the daily loss formula, the drawdown type per program and the consistency rules in one place.
  • A 4.8 TrustScore from tens of thousands of reviews.
  • More platforms: MT4, MT5, cTrader and TradingView.
  • A refundable 2-Step fee and a 90% split on 1-Step and Instant.

Watch out (FTMO):

  • The daily loss is equity-based: open profit and loss count, so a floating loss can breach you.
  • EAs are allowed, but an EA can get an account denied if you exceed the maximum capital allocation rule.
  • A 2-minute news restriction applies on funded Standard accounts.
  • Funded Standard accounts must close positions before any market break longer than 2 hours, which affects weekend holds.
  • The 1-Step best-day rule changed on 12 October 2026: for new orders the best day can be at most 50% of total profit.
  • The 2-Step fee is refunded only at your first Reward request; failed attempts are not refunded.
  • Unreplied negative Trustpilot reviews and an unresolved 2-star complaint (10 November 2026).
  • Instant uses a 10% intraday trailing drawdown that does not reset on withdrawal.
