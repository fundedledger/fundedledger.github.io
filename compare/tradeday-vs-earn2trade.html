TradeDay vs Earn2Trade (2026)

Two monthly-subscription futures programs with simulated accounts. TradeDay is cheaper and allows copiers. Earn2Trade is end-of-day through evaluation and LiveSim, but bans copiers and adds a daily loss limit.

Checked 3 Nov 2026I haven't traded either

Disclosure: this page contains affiliate links. If you sign up through the TradeDay or Earn2Trade links I may earn a commission, at no extra cost to you. I haven't traded TradeDay and I haven't traded Earn2Trade, so this is research from each firm's own pages, not experience.

Short answer: pick TradeDay if price, copiers, no daily loss limit and a free Level 1 data feed matter most, and you accept that its funded drawdown can be intraday. Pick Earn2Trade if you want end-of-day drawdown through evaluation and LiveSim and can live with no copiers, a hard daily loss limit and a 5-profitable-day first payout. Neither is a clear winner on every rule.

Drawdown: where the two differ most

My stance

Intraday trailing drawdown cost me Apex accounts again and again, so I stopped buying it. On futures I only buy end-of-day trailing. Even that has ended funded accounts of mine that were in profit, but it's the best option futures firms offer.

Using that filter, neither firm is clean. TradeDay Quick Pay Intraday is intraday trailing throughout, so it fails my test. Quick Pay EOD is end-of-day in evaluation, but TradeDay's payout policy says the drawdown on all funded Quick Pay accounts is calculated intraday, so it changes after you pass. Fast Pass is labelled end-of-day on its card; I couldn't read an official funded-stage article, so confirm that wording before relying on it.

Earn2Trade is labelled end-of-day in evaluation and LiveSim, but open equity losses count in real time, so a trade that swings against you can breach before the day ends. The Live account moves to trailing drawdown. The hard daily loss limit is a second line you can fail on, and TradeDay doesn't have one.

Payouts

TradeDay. Minimum payout is $250. On Quick Pay the first $4,000 of net profit is split 50/50, then 80/20; Funded Live is 90/10. Fast Pass is 80/20 but needs 5 profitable days and caps each cycle for accounts opened on or after 26 Jul 2026 ($750, $1,500, $1,875 and $2,250 by size). Requests made before the cutoff are processed within about 24 hours on business days (sources differ on whether the cutoff is ET or CT).

Earn2Trade. The split depends on each withdrawal: 50% below a size threshold, 80% at or above it (for example $1,500 on TCP25 and $2,250 on TCP50). Payouts run weekly on Wednesdays, with the request emailed by 2:00pm CT the Friday before. The first withdrawal needs 5 profitable days, with a minimum daily profit of $100 to $350 depending on size. LiveSim has cumulative caps before you move to Live. Earn2Trade also removed its Progression Ladder for subscriptions bought on or after 1 Nov 2026, so new accounts have fixed contract caps; older accounts keep the ladder.

Neither firm's rules guarantee you'll be paid. Both can deny payouts for rule breaches, and both have Trustpilot complaints that I haven't been able to verify.

Watch out: TradeDay

  • Funded drawdown can be intraday. Quick Pay EOD changes to intraday once funded.
  • 50/50 split on the first $4,000 of net profit on Quick Pay.
  • Strict trading rules: day trading only, flat 10 minutes before the close, a Tier 1 news blackout, and bans on bought or shared bots, scalpers over 200 trades a day, hedging and order splitting.
  • Rules change. Fast Pass funded caps and consistency changed on 26 Jul 2026, with older accounts kept on the old terms.
  • No refunds except where the law requires, and it isn't registered with the SEC or CFTC. The accounts are simulated.
  • Payout complaints exist. On the Trustpilot pages I read (3 of 64), a reviewer said funded accounts were disabled after a first payout request; TradeDay replied that denials are for prohibited trade practices. Unverified. Trustpilot showed about 4.6 from 1,453 reviews. I haven't traded it.

Watch out: Earn2Trade

  • Copy trading is banned on every program. Breaking it can cost you accounts or withdrawals, with no refund.
  • Hard daily loss limit and real-time open-equity drawdown, plus Live moving to trailing.
  • Recent rule changes: the Progression Ladder is gone for new subscriptions, and the 5-profitable-day first withdrawal is drawing 1-star Trustpilot reviews from 2 Nov 2026.
  • Extra costs: $139 activation, withdrawal fees, and a 50% split on smaller withdrawals. Your first payout needs profit beyond the activation fee.
  • No refunds, including automatic renewals. It describes itself as an educational company with no financial licence.
  • Pass figure: 8.89% in 2025, by its own count. Trustpilot showed 4.5 from 5,070 reviews; many 1-star reviews are about strict rule enforcement (unverified). I haven't traded it.

FAQ

Is TradeDay or Earn2Trade cheaper?

On the figures I checked on 3 Nov 2026, TradeDay's monthly plans cost less. TradeDay's 50K plans were $59 to $95 a month with its own promo running, against Earn2Trade's $170 a month "starting at" on Gauntlet Mini 50K. Earn2Trade's homepage shows lower "from $75" and "from $85" figures, so sources differ. Check both checkouts.

Can I use a trade copier at TradeDay or Earn2Trade?

TradeDay allows copying across 2 to 10 accounts in evaluation and Funded Sim, with limits. Earn2Trade says trade copiers are not allowed on any of its programs, and breaking that rule can cost you accounts or withdrawals with no refund.

Which one has end-of-day drawdown?

Earn2Trade uses end-of-day drawdown in evaluation and LiveSim, but open losses count in real time and Live accounts are trailing. TradeDay's Quick Pay EOD is end-of-day in evaluation but calculated intraday once funded. TradeDay's Fast Pass card says end-of-day, but I couldn't confirm the funded wording.
